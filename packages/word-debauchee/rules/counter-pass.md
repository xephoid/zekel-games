# Word Debauchee — independent rule enumeration (counter-pass)

Source: `rules.md` only, read by a fresh model with nothing else from the repository (the port-planning runbook §3a); 2026-10-27. Reconciled against the plan's feature inventory in PORT-PLAN.md §14.

| section | rule | detail |
|---|---|---|
| Header | Player count | 2–8 players. |
| Header | Play time | 20–40 minutes. |
| Header | Age | Ages 10+. |
| Header | Premise: roles | One player per round is the debauchee and holds a secret word; everyone else is a guesser. |
| Header | Premise: goal of guessers | Guessers race to spell a synonym of the secret word from the letter tiles; they may talk but may never say the word they are spelling nor tell anyone which letter to grab. |
| Header | Premise: pressure | Every 30 seconds the debauchee lays down one more letter of the secret word and the guessers' payout shrinks. |
| Header | Premise: winner | "The best debauchee wins" (the Winning section later says highest total score wins). |
| Components | Letter tiles | 144 tiles; distribution in the appendix. |
| Components | Word cards | 500 cards; one word each, with its facts and its synonym list. |
| Components | Clue cards | 48 cards in two decks: 20 number clues and 28 word clues (list in the appendix). |
| Components | Sand timer | 1 timer, 30 seconds. |
| Components | Score pad | 1. |
| Components: a word card | Card layout | Top half = facts: letters, vowels, consonants, syllables, category, type, grade, difficulty (shown as dots, e.g. ●○○). Bottom half = synonym list (HAPPY example: glad, merry, jolly, joyful, cheerful, content, pleased, upbeat). |
| Components: a word card | Facts answer clue cards | The facts exist so the debauchee can answer any clue card without counting or judging. |
| Components: a word card | Synonym list is the referee | A spelled word scores if and only if it is on the card's synonym list. |
| Components: a word card | Vowel definition | A, E, I, O, U are vowels; Y is a consonant. |
| Components: a word card | Grade definition | Grade = the US school grade the word is usually taught in. |
| Components: a clue card | Clue card obligation | Each clue card names one thing the debauchee must tell the table about the secret word. |
| Components: a clue card | Two clue kinds | Number clues have a number for an answer (e.g. Letters, Vowels, Grade, Shortest synonym); word clues do not (e.g. Category, First letter, Rhymes with, Opposite). |
| Setup | Pool | Tip all 144 tiles face up in the middle of the table and spread them so everyone can reach them; this is the pool. |
| Setup | Build area | Leave an open space beside the pool; this is the build area. |
| Setup | Word deck | Shuffle the word cards into one face-down deck. |
| Setup | Clue decks | Shuffle the number clues and the word clues into two separate face-down decks. |
| Setup | First debauchee | Whoever most recently got away with something. |
| Round 1: pick a word | Draw three | The debauchee draws three word cards and secretly chooses one. |
| Round 1: pick a word | Return two | The two unchosen cards are slid under the word deck (bottom). |
| Round 1: pick a word | Keep hidden | The chosen card stays hidden. |
| Round 2: give clues | Draw four clues | The debauchee draws two number clues and two word clues and looks at them in secret. |
| Round 2: give clues | Play three | The debauchee chooses three of the four to play and lays them face up where everyone can see them. |
| Round 2: give clues | Answer truthfully | The debauchee answers each played clue out loud, truthfully. |
| Round 2: give clues | Guaranteed mix | Because 3 of 4 (2+2) are played, at least one number clue and at least one word clue are always among the played three. |
| Round 2: give clues | Fourth card | The unplayed fourth clue card goes under its own deck. |
| Round 2: give clues | Debauchee says nothing more | Nothing else may be said about the word, now or later. |
| Round 2: give clues | Repeat on request | Any guesser may ask the debauchee to repeat an answer at any time. |
| Round 2: give clues | Flip timer | After the clues, the debauchee flips the timer. |
| Round 3: build | Turn order | Play passes clockwise among the guessers, starting with the player to the debauchee's left, cycling until the round ends. |
| Round 3: build | One action per turn | On your turn you do exactly one of: Place, Move/Return, or Call "Reveal!". |
| Round 3: build | Action: Place | Place one tile from the pool into the build area. |
| Round 3: build | Action: Move | Move one tile already in the build area (to another spot in the build area). |
| Round 3: build | Action: Return | Instead of moving it, return one tile from the build area to the pool (same action bullet as Move). |
| Round 3: build | Action: Call "Reveal!" | The debauchee immediately reveals a letter (as in step 4) and restarts the timer. Intended for when you'd rather have more information than more time. |
| Round 3: build | No passing | You may not pass. |
| Round 3: build | Free layout | Build wherever you like; several candidate words can sit in the build area at once. |
| Round 3: build | Guessers talk freely | Guessers may talk freely, subject to two exceptions. |
| Round 3: build | Never say the target word | A guesser must never say the word they want spelled. |
| Round 3: build | Burned words | Any word a guesser says out loud is burned and cannot score this round, even if it is on the synonym list. |
| Round 3: build | Hinting allowed | Hinting around a word is allowed (e.g. "the opposite of sad", "what a dog does with a bone"). |
| Round 3: build | Never suggest a letter | A guesser must never tell or suggest a letter to another player: no "starts with G", no "you need an L", no pointing at tiles. |
| Round 3: build | Debauchee stays silent | The debauchee may listen, smirk and say nothing. |
| Round 3: build | No confirm/deny | The debauchee never confirms or denies guesses about the secret word. |
| Round 3: build | Calling "Check!" | When your tile completes a word you believe is a synonym, say "Check!" and point at it. |
| Round 3: build | Check answer | The debauchee looks at the card and answers only "yes" or "no". |
| Round 3: build | What counts as a word | A word reads only if its tiles sit touching, in one straight line, left to right. |
| Round 3: build | After a "no" | A "no" word may stay on the table; players can keep moving its tiles. |
| Round 4: timer runs out | Finish current turn | When the sand runs out, the current turn finishes first. |
| Round 4: timer runs out | Reveal a letter | Then the debauchee reveals: takes one tile from the pool matching a letter of the secret word and places it face up in a row in front of them. |
| Round 4: timer runs out | Debauchee picks the letter | The debauchee chooses which letter of the secret word to reveal; positions within the word are not shown. |
| Round 4: timer runs out | Pool empty of that letter | If the pool has no tile of that letter, the debauchee takes one from the build area instead. |
| Round 4: timer runs out | Resume | Then the debauchee flips the timer and play resumes with the next guesser in turn order. |
| Round 5: round ends | End: cracked | The round ends the instant a check gets a "yes". |
| Round 5: round ends | End: stumped | The round ends the instant the debauchee places the last letter of the secret word in front of them (i.e. revealed tiles = letters in the word). |
| Round 5: round ends | Reveal the word | The debauchee turns the word card face up and reads the word. |
| Scoring | Stumped | The debauchee scores the number of letters in the secret word; guessers score nothing. |
| Scoring | Cracked | Every guesser scores (letters in the cracked synonym) minus (tiles the debauchee has revealed), minimum 1; the debauchee scores nothing. |
| Scoring | Worked examples | HAPPY cracked with GLAD, 0 revealed: guessers 4 each, debauchee 0. Cracked with CHEERFUL, 2 revealed: 6 each. Cracked with GLAD, 4 revealed: 1 each (floor). Never cracked, 5 revealed: guessers 0, debauchee 5. |
| Scoring | Record scores | Write the scores down. |
| Scoring | Reset tiles | Return every tile to the pool. |
| Scoring | Next debauchee | The player to the debauchee's left becomes the next debauchee. |
| Winning | Game length | The game ends once every player has been the debauchee the same number of times: once each with 5–8 players, twice each with 2–4 players. |
| Winning | Winner | Highest total score wins. |
| Winning | Ties | Ties share the win. |
| Example round | Clue selection | Ann draws Letters, Shortest synonym, Type, Opposite; buries Shortest synonym (would reveal "4"); plays "Five letters. It's an adjective. The opposite is SAD." |
| Example round | Cracking | Ben G, Cat ("oh, like the opposite of sad?") L, Dev A, Ben D then "Check!"; GLAD is listed, "Yes." 0 revealed, so Ben, Cat, Dev score 4 each, Ann 0; Ben (Ann's left) is next debauchee. |
| Example round | Slower round | Reveal P at 30 s, second P at 60 s, then Dev calls "Reveal!" and Ann lays down Y (repeated letters are revealed as separate tiles); Cat finishes MERRY; each guesser scores 5 − 3 = 2. |
| Variants | Laps instead of sand | Drop the timer; the debauchee reveals a letter each time the turn comes back round to the first guesser. Recommended for 7–8 players, where 30 seconds can give some players no turn. |
| Variants | Bribe | When a guesser calls "Reveal!", the guessers may take a clue instead of a letter: the caller draws the top card of either clue deck and the debauchee answers it. A bribed clue counts as a revealed tile for scoring. |
| Variants | Open hand | Kinder for new players: skip the clue draw; the debauchee chooses any three clue cards from the whole set, at least one of them a number clue. |
| Variants | Two-player duel | One guesser, no turn order: they place tiles freely while the sand runs. Play four rounds each. |
| Variants | The House | Digital edition: the computer is the debauchee every round; everyone at the table guesses as one team against it; a shared screen is fine (nobody at the table has a secret). Play as many rounds as a normal game at that table size; highest total wins, the table or the house. |

## Silent or vague

1. **Who may call "Check!", and when.** "When your tile completes a word" reads as: only the player whose Place or Move just completed the word, on their own turn. Alternative reading: anyone may check any completed word at any time. Also unstated whether a word completed by a Move (not a Place) can be checked, and whether a word completed earlier but never checked can be checked on a later turn.
2. **Is Check a free action?** Check is not one of the three turn actions, so it appears free and follows the action; the rulebook never says whether a "no" ends the turn, or whether a player may check more than one word after one placement.
3. **Burned word that gets checked.** If guessers spell a word one of them said aloud, the text says it "cannot score" but does not say what the debauchee answers: "yes" (it is on the list) with no score and an ended round, or "no". Two readings. Nobody is assigned to track burned words.
4. **Scope of "burned".** Literally every word a guesser utters is burned, including incidental words ("the", "sad" when quoting the Opposite clue). Alternative reading: only words offered as the target. The rulebook's "even if it is on the list" suggests the literal reading, but a guesser repeating the debauchee's own clue answer (e.g. an Opposite or Goes-with word that happens to be on the synonym list) is not addressed.
5. **Words the debauchee says.** Rhymes-with explicitly excludes list words, but Opposite and Goes-with do not; nothing says whether a list word spoken by the debauchee in a clue answer is burned or still scores.
6. **Spelling the secret word itself.** "Scores if and only if it is on the list" implies spelling HAPPY itself is a "no" (the headword is not on its own list), but the rulebook never says so outright, and a table may expect it to crack.
7. **"Nothing else may be said about the word, now or later."** Context implies this binds the debauchee only (guessers plainly discuss the word via hints); as written it could be read as binding everyone.
8. **Sub-runs within a line.** If tiles read G-L-A-D-E in one line, does GLAD "read"? The straight-line rule can be read as the whole contiguous run being the word, or as any contiguous sub-run. Crossing/shared-tile layouts are likewise unaddressed.
9. **Reveal from the build area.** When the pool lacks the letter, the debauchee takes one from the build area; which tile (if several) and whether this may break a candidate word are unstated (presumably debauchee's choice).
10. **Revealing with letter multiplicity.** The example (P, P, Y) shows repeated letters are revealed one tile per occurrence, but the main rule only says "a letter of the secret word"; it never states that the row must eventually be exactly the multiset of the word's letters, which the "last letter" end condition depends on.
11. **Timer expiring during a "Reveal!" turn.** If the sand runs out while a player is calling "Reveal!", step 4 ("current turn finishes, then reveal") and the Reveal! action ("reveals ... and restarts the timer") could yield one or two reveals.
12. **Unanswerable word clues.** The debauchee must play at least one word clue; Opposite (e.g. for VILLAGE) or Goes-with may have no honest answer. Only Rhymes-with has an escape ("nothing rhymes"). Register and Goes-with are not among the card facts, so they are judged, not read.
13. **First vowel with no vowel.** Since Y is a consonant, words like GYM or RHYTHM have no first vowel; the card's answer is undefined (and Vowels would be 0).
14. **Difficulty clue vs card dots.** The clue says "1–3"; the card shows ●○○ dots. The mapping (1 dot = 1) is implied, not stated.
15. **Where played cards go.** The three played clue cards and the used word card are never said to be returned, buried or discarded at round end (only the fourth clue and the two rejected word cards are placed under their decks).
16. **Two-player game length.** Winning says 2–4 players play twice each (4 rounds for 2 players); the Two-player duel variant says "four rounds each" (8 rounds). Either the variant overrides, or base-rules 2-player is a distinct mode — the base rules with one guesser are never otherwise distinguished from the duel.
17. **Bribe variant details.** "The guessers may take a clue" — unclear whether the caller or the group decides; unclear whether the timer still restarts (the Reveal! it replaces restarts it); unclear what happens if the drawn clue duplicates one already played, and where the bribed card goes afterward.
18. **Laps variant details.** Whether a reveal happens at the start of every lap after the first (i.e. before the first guesser's second turn), whether "Reveal!" still exists, and whether a called reveal resets the lap are all unstated.
19. **Open hand duplicates.** "Any three clue cards from the whole set" does not say whether three copies of the same card (e.g. Letters ×3) are allowed; the draw mechanic normally prevents this only by chance.
20. **The House turn order.** With no debauchee at the table, who the first guesser is (the "player to the debauchee's left") is undefined; also whether the team scores once per round (as one guesser would) is implied by "as one team" but not stated.
21. **Synonyms the tile set cannot spell / multiword synonyms.** With fixed tile counts (e.g. Z 2, Q 2) some list entries may be unspellable, and hyphenated or two-word list entries cannot sit "in one straight line" of tiles; the rulebook never says whether such entries are excluded from lists or simply unreachable.
22. **Premise vs Winning.** The intro says "the best debauchee wins" while Winning says highest total score wins, which includes points earned as a guesser; a reader could take the intro as a separate tiebreak or objective. (Not a mechanical gap, but two readings of the goal.)
