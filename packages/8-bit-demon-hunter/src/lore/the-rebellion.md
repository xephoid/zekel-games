---
title: The Rebellion
summary: The champions of Chineera, the rebel group that fought the demon, built towns, and left names, coins and a symbol behind.
tags: history, rebellion, towns, symbol
---

**Who they were.** The champions of Chineera: the people who stood with her against the demon that wore Memlax. Farmers and soldiers, a few wizards, a great many who did not come back. They were called rebels because, at the time, Memlax was the law.

**What they left.** Towns: at least one of the five was built by rebels, and the oldest houses in the old towns all face the same way, away from the ruins to the east, as if whoever built them feared something specific. A symbol: a broken chain, or maybe a flame, still scratched into old walls and, lately, drawn by children who do not know where they learned it. Coins with a woman's face on them. Lists of names, kept by some families for generations so the dead are not forgotten; half the children born in the towns for a hundred years have been named after people on those lists, and nobody planned it. Pedestals: five of them in the ruins outside town, all empty, that once held something important.

**The ruins.** Everyone is told to be careful around the old ruins to the east. Nobody remembers why. They keep saying it.

**What is not known.** Whether the rebellion forged the five artifacts or only used them. Who exactly is on the lists. What the symbol means.

What the townsfolk say:

- A group of rebels built this town a long time ago. No idea what they were rebelling against.
- The champions of Chineera were a rebel group. One of my ancestors was a member.
- My family's been keeping a list of names for generations: people who fought in some old rebellion.
- The old rebellion against the demon had a symbol, a broken chain or maybe a flame.
- My boy keeps drawing the same symbol over and over. The old woman down the road says it's the mark of the ancient rebellion.
- I once found an old coin with a woman's face on it. The merchant said it was minted by the rebellion.
- I had an uncle who spent his whole life researching the old rebellion. Thirty years of notes; my cousin used them to start a fire.
- The oldest buildings in town all have their doors facing the same direction: away from the old ruins.
- Watch yourself around those old ruins to the east. Nobody remembers why anymore, but we keep saying it.
- Half the children born in this town for the last hundred years have been named after people from the old rebellion. Nobody planned it.
