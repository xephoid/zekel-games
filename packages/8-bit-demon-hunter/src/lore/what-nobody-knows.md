---
title: What nobody knows (for the GM)
summary: The questions the world cannot answer, so the GM keeps them open instead of inventing.
tags: gm, mystery, limits
---

Players ask open questions. These are the ones this world has no answer to. When one comes up, the person being asked does not know, the old stories disagree, or the answer is lost; never make one up.

- **The demon's true name and face.** Nobody has seen it. Some say it has no face of its own, only the ones it borrows.
- **What Memlax gave up in his bargain.** Everyone agrees it was something awful; nobody agrees what. The stories offer a soul, a memory, a child, his name; none is confirmed.
- **Whether Chineera's illness was the demon's doing.** Half the towns say the demon poisoned her; half say she just got sick.
- **How Chineera pushed the demon out.** The songs say love; the scholars say five artifacts; the rebels' lists say a lot of people died. All three may be true.
- **Where the demon went, and why it came back.** It was "pushed out", not killed. Where it was between then and now, and what let it back in, nobody can say. Some blame forgetting.
- **How a person becomes a minion.** Some say the demon offers a deal like the one it offered Memlax. Some say it needs no consent. The minions themselves say nothing.
- **What the bindings are.** Ten named relics the minions keep. What each one actually does, who made them, and why they weaken the demon are not known; only that they do.
- **Who forged the five artifacts, and when.** "Long ago", "at the barren places", "by the rebellion", "by Chineera herself": every town has a version.
- **Why the temples stand where they do,** and who built the gauntlets and the riddle doors.
- **Whether the monsters can be driven off for good.** They came with the demon; the hope is they leave with it.
- **Anything about the player's own past.** The player is a stranger who came to hunt the demon. Townsfolk may wonder aloud why; they do not know.

When a player presses for one of these, the right answer is a shrug, a legend offered as a legend, or a different person who "might know more", never a fact.
