# Strategy guide — 8-bit Demon Hunter

For the integration-harness player (zekel `docs/integration-runbook.md`).
Strategy only — the assistant and the game server are the rules authority;
when unsure what is legal, ask. Sources: PORT-PLAN.md rulings and the
designer's playtests (seeds 33–99).

## How this game is played

- This is a **solo, fully digital** game. There is no physical box, deck or
  die: the assistant runs the whole world through the game server and reports
  everything. **Never use ASK-TABLE** — nothing at your table decides anything.
- Your "hand" is the status the assistant reports: HP, stats, shuckles (money),
  resources, bombs, lockpicks, magic items, bindings, active tasks and clues.
  Keep a NOTE line of it after every few moves so you notice if it drifts.
- Setup: ask for a new game of this exact game, give your name. There is no
  role, colour or difficulty to choose. Ask for the rules once at the start and
  for a lore topic or two if curious; after that, play.
- Decisions that are YOURS and that nobody may make for you: which stat to
  raise at a level-up, which tasks to accept, how much to buy or sell, where to
  travel, fight or flee, whether to confirm a bomb that would hurt you, riddle
  or gauntlet at a temple, whom to search, accuse or stab, and what (if
  anything) you say to the demon. The assistant must never hint at who the
  demon is or what to do — if it does, flag it.
- One thing per message, but you may ask for several plain moves at once
  ("talk to everyone here, then accept every task") when nothing in between
  needs a decision from you.

## What wins

The demon is hiding as one of the townsfolk. You win by naming the right
person and killing it in its lair. Three things decide the finale:

1. **Identify it.** Clues come from talking to people (the Carpenter's
   certainties rule towns out; minions lie and their lies point at the demon),
   from rewards for finished tasks, from secrets, and from truth-telling
   items. Keep a list of who it cannot be; cross off towns and trades.
2. **Weaken it.** Every **binding** (found by searching houses) and every
   **magic artifact** (won at a temple) takes a large bite out of the demon's
   HP. Fresh from the start you cannot kill it at all. Aim for **five or six
   weakenings** before you stab; eight makes the lair easy.
3. **Be strong enough to survive the lair.** It summons monsters at two thirds
   HP and hits harder in its last third. Strength 4+, agility 12+, health 6+,
   range 2+ is the comfortable floor.

Dying in the lair is not game over: the knife turns time back and you keep
all you learned. Dying outside is the same. Death costs tempo, not the game.

## Opening (first 50 turns)

- Talk to **everyone** in the starting town and accept **every** task. Finds
  and escorts are cheap experience and money; the rewards give clues.
- Raise **health first** (you start at 3 HP, which two hits kill), then
  **range to 2** (you strike first against most early enemies) and
  **strength**. Agility comes later. Raising health also heals you fully.
- Farm the nearest clearings: slimes, mushrooms and lone bandits are safe
  experience. Sell harvests in bulk at the inn; buy nothing but a sleep when
  low and, later, lockpicks and bombs.
- Leave the town only with full HP. Sleep at the inn when below half.

## Mid-game habits

- **Never fight at low HP.** Most deaths come from one more fight at 2 HP.
- **Fight what your noise attracts; never flee from Skeletons or Wizards.**
  They hit you as you run. Flee only from things slower than you.
- **Undead Knights and crowds of five or more mushrooms**: leave, unless you
  are near your caps.
- Finish tasks for **minions** (the townsfolk who serve the demon): when a
  minion's task is done it confesses, and a confessed minion's house is
  searchable for free. Those houses hold bindings. Minions ask for hard
  things (kill tasks); do them when you are strong enough, not before.
- Use **favours**: a Blacksmith favour is a free stat point, a Locksmith
  favour opens a house. Claim rewards as soon as a task completes.
- **Travel only to places you know.** Learn the map by walking the clearings
  around each town once; later, take carts from the inn to skip walking.
- Keep a **rule-out list**: towns and trades the demon cannot be. The game
  rewards elimination far more than guessing.

## Temples (artifacts)

Each temple offers a riddle (free if you can answer it) or a gauntlet of four
guardians. In rough order of difficulty for a mid-game hunter:

- **Fire Temple** — easy with strength 5 and range 2 (one hit each).
- **Space Temple** — Arachnes are soft at strength 4, range 2. Its artifact,
  the Cape of Teleportation, is the best in the game: it jumps you to any
  visited town **and heals you to full**. Get it as early as you can.
- **Sky Temple** — its wall needs a bomb; its Bees need agility 16 to be safe.
- **Earth and Light Temples** — hard; take the riddle or leave them for last.

## Endgame

- Stab only when you are sure of the name, have five or more weakenings, and
  are at full HP. Teleport or sleep first.
- If the lair goes badly, die, come back stronger: one more binding, one more
  artifact, one more level. Do not grind for the level cap; weakenings beat
  stats.
- Expect the whole hunt to take a few hundred moves. That is normal.
