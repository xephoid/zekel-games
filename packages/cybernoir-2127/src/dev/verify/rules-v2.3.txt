1. Overview
A crime has been committed in Dark City, and the
Detective knows exactly who did it — because the
Detective is the perpetrator. They've pinned the crime on the
Hacker , and now they're racing to "arrest" them before
the truth comes out.

The Detective wins by deducing the Hacker's hidden
location and making the arrest.
The Hacker wins by assembling all 3 pieces
of Evidence (Weapon, Witnesses, Motive) that clear their name —
and surviving the Detective's final desperate guess.

The core tension: the Hacker must interact with people around the
city to build their case, but every person they use might secretly be
one of the Detective's informants — and getting burned
by an informant leaks a Clue to the Detective about
where the Hacker is hiding.

3. The Hidden Location &
Clues
The Hacker's hideout is defined by three attributes, each matching a
Clue token type:

Borough (3 possible values)
Population (0, 1, 2, or 3)
Affiliation (6 possible values)

When the Detective gains a Clue , the Hacker
chooses which of the three categories to reveal truthfully (e.g., hands
over the "Downtown" Borough token if hiding Downtown). This keeps the
Hacker in control of what leaks, but every leak narrows the search.
No repeats: each truthful clue category can be given
only once. When the Detective earns a Clue, the Hacker must choose a
category not yet revealed. Note that Borough + Affiliation alone
uniquely identify a location, so the third truthful clue only confirms
what the first two imply. If the Detective earns a Clue when all three
categories are already revealed, it has no effect.
A Negative Clue is a token the Hacker declares
false (e.g., "my location's affiliation is NOT Crimson Clan"). A
Negative Clue must be new information : a token not previously
declared false and not belonging to a category already truthfully
revealed.

4. Setup

The Hacker secretly chooses their hideout from the
18 locations, and takes that Location card out of the Location
deck , keeping it facedown in front of them as their
safehouse card (v1.7). The Location deck is therefore 17 cards.

Consequences worth knowing: a face-up Location on the board can
never be the hideout, and a Detective who tracks every card they have
seen (hand, board, discards) narrows the hideout by elimination — if
the deck runs out, the missing card gives it away. Burn the Safehouse
(§5.2a) is the Hacker's once-per-game answer.

The Hacker secretly gathers the Contacts cards for the people
resident at that location (0–3 cards).

Etiquette rule: always go through the motions of searching the
Contacts deck, even if your location has population 0 — otherwise you
leak information immediately. This is also why the Detective gets no
special knowledge of how the starting hand was assembled.

The Hacker shuffles the remaining Contacts deck (which includes the
Weapon ) and draws until they hold 3
cards total (residents + random draws).

The Detective shuffles the Location deck and draws
3 .

The Detective takes the POI deck for designating informants and
tracking arrests.

5.2a Burn the Safehouse (XXX, once per
game — v1.8)
The Hacker abandons the compromised hideout and disappears into a new
one. Resolve in this order:

The Hacker collects the Detective's Location discard
pile and the remaining Location deck into one
pile, and adds their old safehouse card to it. (Cards in
the Detective's hand and face up on the board stay where they are.)
The Hacker secretly chooses a different location from
that pile and takes its card as the new safehouse card.
The Hacker shuffles the rest of the pile facedown — it becomes the new
Location deck.
Clear the clues: every Clue token on the table — truthful and
Negative — is returned to the supply. The Detective's
investigation restarts from zero; clue categories can be earned
again as normal. (v1.7 instead swapped the truthful tokens to the
new hideout's values; v1.8 replaces that with the full clear.)

Notes: the burn costs the Hacker's whole normal turn (3 AP) unless
they Overclock first. Gathering the pile shows the Hacker every card in
it — so, by elimination, which Locations the Detective is holding. The
returned discards also push back the deck-exhaustion endgame (§5.3).
Because every clue is wiped, the burn is at its strongest late — the more
the Detective has learned, the more a burn destroys. The Detective's only
durable knowledge across a burn is card elimination: locations they have
seen in hand, on the board, or in their discards before the burn (minus
what went back into the pile) — and the burn returns the discards, so
even that mostly resets.
Contacts deck: if the Contacts deck runs out,
reshuffle the discard pile into a new deck. The Hacker can and will
cycle through the full deck over the course of a game. There is
no hand limit for the Hacker.

Card flow: a person played for their ability goes to
the Hacker's discard after resolving (retrievable via Shizuoka Inc or the
reshuffle). A card played as Evidence stays on the table for the rest of
the game.
5.3 Endgame triggers
Two events force the final showdown. In both cases the Detective gets
one final Location guess , granted even if the mid-game
guess was already spent. Correct = the Detective wins; wrong = the
Hacker is exonerated and wins.

Evidence complete: the moment the Hacker completes
all 3 pieces of Evidence, the Detective makes the final guess
immediately — the Hacker's turn ends on the spot, and
nothing else resolves first.

Location deck exhausted: if the Detective cannot
make their mandatory start-of-turn draw, the city has gone cold. The
Detective must make the final guess by the end of that turn. (The deck
never reshuffles, so every board discard, forced hand discard, bought
Clue, and Overclock draw quietly shortens the game — a built-in
timer. The deck starts at 17 since v1.7 — the Hacker holds their
safehouse card — and the one exception to "never reshuffles" is Burn the
Safehouse (§5.2a), which folds the discard pile back in and resets some
of the timer.)

6. Informants

The Detective recruits an informant by paying XXX
and placing a POI card facedown — only a person
at a face-up location they have played (v2.3
clarification, same reach as an Arrest).
Each subsequent Detective turn, every informant costs 1 AP
upkeep (paid after the mandatory location draw and play) or
must be released.
Anansi the Spider (v2.0): when the Hacker plays
Anansi as Evidence, they remove one informant of their choice — a
facedown one by pointing at its position (it returns to the POI deck
unseen ) or a revealed one by name. Blocking Anansi's
Evidence commit is therefore a premium block.
Iceden rider (v2.0, restated for v2.1 set play):
when the Hacker plays a Motive set containing an Iceden
Collective member , the gang springs a locked contact — the
Hacker removes one revealed informant of their choice,
free (the Win Back effect with no Contact card spent). Once per set (a
set holds at most one Iceden member — affiliations are distinct). Facedown informants are out of
reach. If the set completes all 3 Evidence, the final guess happens
immediately and the rider is skipped. If there is no revealed
informant, nothing happens. A set holding BOTH Anansi and an Iceden
member resolves Anansi's removal first, then the win-back.
When the Hacker plays a person (for an ability or as Evidence)
matching a facedown informant, the Detective may reveal
it to block the play. Blocking earns the Detective a
Clue (Hacker's choice of category).
A revealed informant stays in play face up — it
continues to block that person and still requires upkeep. The Detective
may release any informant (revealed or not) on their turn by declining
upkeep.
Winning back a revealed informant: the Hacker may
play the matching Contact for its printed cost (Witnesses cost
X ) to remove a revealed informant — or get
one free via the Iceden rider above —
thematically, convincing the contact to stop ratting. The POI card
returns facedown to the hidden POI deck (the Detective may re-recruit it
later at full XXX cost), and the Hacker's Contact goes to their discard
— it does not count as Evidence. Facedown informants
cannot be targeted this way. This rule exists to break a deadlock:
without it, the Detective could hold enough Witnesses as informants
indefinitely to lock the Hacker out of the 3 Witness cards their
Evidence requires.

OmniSuperUltra Corp's "Reveal an informant": the Hacker chooses
any one facedown informant and flips it face up. The revealed informant
behaves exactly like one the Detective revealed: it keeps blocking its
person and keeps costing upkeep. The ability's value is intel —
the Hacker learns which person is burned and can route around it, while
the Detective must keep paying for a scouted asset or release it.
The POI deck is hidden. POI cards only leave it to
become informants or to sit on the jail track; whenever a person is
released from either, their card goes back facedown into the hidden
deck. The Hacker therefore only ever knows where a person was last
seen — a released informant vanishes back into anonymity and could
be re-recruited (or arrested) later without the Hacker knowing.

7. Evidence (Hacker's Win
Objectives)
The Hacker must establish all three:

The Weapon — 1 unique card shuffled into the
Contacts deck. Find it and play it (free, like all Evidence). The Weapon
has no POI copy, so it can never be an informant, never be blocked, and
never be arrested.
The Witnesses — reveal/play 3 Witness
cards (all 7 "None"-affiliation people are Witnesses —
v2.0 adds Anansi the Spider, the only Witness with an ability).
The Motive — play 3 sets of 3 people
(v1.9). Each set holds 3 unique people from 3 DIFFERENT
affiliations — any affiliations, including None (Witnesses),
with at most TWO gangs per set (at least one member
must be a citizen or corporate voice — v2.3; the v1.9 one-gang cap was
measured far too harsh under complete-trio play, and no cap far too
loose), and no two sets may use the same three
affiliations (each completed set's affiliation trio must be
unique; 19 of the 20 affiliation trios are legal — only the all-gang trio is not). v2.1: a set
is played as a COMPLETE trio — all 3 cards at once, in a single
action. Motive cards are never banked one at a time; there are no
partial sets on the table. Completed sets may be played in
any order. Each card of the set gets the informant check (§5.2/§6), in
declared order — ONE accepted block bounces the WHOLE set back
to the Hacker's hand (the informant is revealed and the
Detective earns the Clue as usual; declining every check lets the set
land). A set containing an Iceden Collective member
fires the gang's rider (§6) once. (v1.6–v1.8: two
sets of one-per-all-6-affiliations, played sequentially — replaced
because it made Witnesses the choke point of the whole Evidence race:
5 of the 6 None people were required across Evidence, which is what
informant blocks and arrests squeezed.)

No double-counting: a card counts toward exactly one
piece of Evidence. Witnesses used in a None motive slot are separate cards
from the 3 played as Witness evidence — but under v1.9 the Hacker can
avoid None motive slots entirely, so only 3 of the 6 None people are
required.
Played Evidence is safe: it can't be arrested and can't be an
informant.

9. Edge-Case Rulings

Empty Detective hand is legal — and never blocks
play. "Discard 3 Locations for a Clue" typically empties the
hand, but since the mandatory draw precedes the mandatory play each
turn, the Detective always has a card to play. The real cost of a bought
Clue is losing hand flexibility (choice of which location to
play) and the Iceden Collective discard-from-hand attacks having nothing to hit.
Location cards in hand have no other mechanical function.
No Evidence double-counting (§7): each played card
serves exactly one Evidence requirement.
Contacts deck reshuffles (§5.2): an empty Contacts
deck is never a wall — reshuffle the discard. The Location deck, by
contrast, never reshuffles (§5.3).
Iceden Collective's forced discard is random: when a Iceden Collective
ability forces the Detective to discard a Location from hand, the Hacker
pulls a card blindly from the Detective's (hidden) hand.
People are unique individuals. Contact/POI matching
works by card identity, so in the final game each of the 24 people is a
distinct named character — the three OmniSuperUltra Corp people share an ability but
are not interchangeable copies.
Exhausted clues: a Clue earned after all three
truthful categories are revealed has no effect (§3) — at that point the
Detective knows the location and only needs the AP (or an endgame
trigger) to call it.

11. Variant — Hacker Crew (3–4
Players)
Untested table variant, added 2026-08-13. Every base rule is
unchanged; a crew game is played and tracked as a normal 2-player
session.
Three or four players: one Detective , and the other
2–3 players jointly playing the single Hacker side as a
crew . The crew shares everything the Hacker owns — the
hideout (all crew members know it), the Contacts deck, the hand, the
Evidence zone — and wins or loses together. The Hacker turn is still
exactly 3 AP with the same actions. What changes is who may say
yes : every AP and every card in the crew's hand has an individual
owner, and using it requires that owner's consent.
11.1 The Main Token

Give the Main token to a random crew member at
setup. It passes clockwise to the next crew member at the start of
every Hacker turn.
The Main holder receives every leftover : any AP or
card share not assigned by a rule below (the odd AP with a crew of 2,
the Overclock bonus AP, a Zero Kelvin / Permafrost card no single
contributor can claim).

11.2 Action Points

Each Hacker turn, the 3 AP are owned individually. Crew of
2: the Main holder owns 2, the other crew member owns 1.
Crew of 3: 1 each.
Any crew member may physically take any action, but each AP spent
needs its owner's agreement — an owner may veto the
spend. Actions costing more than 1 AP pool AP from several owners, and
every contributor must agree.
Overclock's bonus AP belongs to the Main holder.
Unspent AP is lost at end of turn, as normal.

11.3 Cards

The crew's hand is one shared pool, hidden from the Detective as
usual, but each card has an owner. Playing a card requires its owner's
agreement (the same veto as AP).
Opening hand (3 cards): with a crew of 2 the Main
holder takes 2 and the other takes 1; with a crew of 3, 1 each.
Draw Contact: the card belongs to whoever's AP paid
for the draw.
Zero Kelvin / Permafrost (draw 3): the owner of the
played person card takes 2 of the drawn cards; the remaining card goes
to the crew member who contributed the AP for the play. If the owner
paid all the AP themselves, they take all 3; if no single other
contributor can claim it (two other crew members each chipped in 1),
the card is a leftover and goes to the Main holder.

11.4 Communication

Whispering is allowed: the crew may confer
privately at the table at any time. The Detective sees that a
conference happens — and how long it takes — but never its
content.
